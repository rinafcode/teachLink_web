# Moderation Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how TeachLink Web moderates user-generated content
and community spaces. It states the principles moderators follow, the
actions they may take, and the transparency they owe, so contributors
and users share one predictable, versioned reference for moderation.

## Scope

This policy applies to moderation of user-generated content and
community interaction in this repository: comments, discussions,
issues, pull request threads, and other public contributions published
through TeachLink Web surfaces.

It is the general moderation reference. Comment-level criteria,
reporting flow, and appeal path remain in
`Governance/domains/COMMENT_MODERATION.md`, and conduct appeals follow
the Code of Conduct process where one exists. Where this policy and a
narrower document conflict, this policy governs the principles, the
graduated actions, and the transparency minimums below.

## Moderation Principles

Moderation follows these principles in every decision:

- **Fairness.** Similar content receives similar treatment. Personal
  views, affiliation, or contributor status do not change the outcome.
- **Proportionality and least-intrusive means.** Moderators use the
  smallest effective action first and escalate only when the content
  or pattern requires it.
- **Consistency and predictability.** Decisions follow the published
  criteria and this policy, not individual judgement alone, so outcomes
  can be explained against the same rules.
- **Safety first.** Content that threatens safety, promotes harm or
  violence, or violates applicable law is acted on promptly, even when
  context is incomplete.
- **Accountability and appealability.** Every moderation decision can
  be reviewed. The affected party is told what was decided, why, and
  how to appeal.
- **Privacy-preserving.** Moderation discloses the minimum personal
  detail needed to explain a decision and keeps reports and private
  evidence confidential.

No principle overrides safety or legal compliance. Trade-offs between
principles are explained in the decision record.

## Actions Available to Moderators

Moderators act through a graduated ladder. They start low and move up
only for severe, repeated, or unremedied cases:

- **Reminder and guidance.** A public or private note that names the
  rule, links the relevant policy, and asks for correction without
  removing content.
- **Warning.** A recorded warning to the contributor that a further
  violation may lead to removal or restriction.
- **Hide or remove content.** Content that clearly violates the
  published criteria may be hidden or removed. The removal states the
  criterion violated and the policy reference.
- **Rate-limit or temporary restriction.** A time-boxed limit on
  posting or interacting, used when warnings and removals do not stop
  the pattern. The duration and the condition for lifting it are
  stated up front.
- **Referral to conduct enforcement.** Serious or sustained violations
  are referred to the community conduct enforcement path in addition
  to content action. Moderators do not impose permanent removals alone;
  permanent removals require a conduct enforcement decision.

Every action above the reminder is recorded with the content reference,
the criterion violated, the action taken, the moderator, and the date.
Knowingly false reports are themselves a violation and are handled as
a conduct matter, not as ordinary content.

## Transparency Expectations

Moderation is transparent by default and confidential where safety or
privacy requires it:

- **Decision notice.** The affected contributor is told what content
  was acted on, which criterion it violated, what action was taken,
  and how to appeal. The reporter receives status feedback on the
  outcome where permitted.
- **Private by default for personal detail.** Reports, reporter
  identity, and private evidence stay confidential. Public spaces carry
  only the fact and reason for an action, never names or private
  details, unless all affected parties agree.
- **Auditable record.** Each enforcement action keeps the minimum
  record needed for accountability: the decision, the reasoning, and
  any follow-up. Records are retained by maintainers and are not
  disclosed beyond those who need them for review or appeal.
- **Aggregate reporting.** Moderation activity is summarized in
  aggregate transparency reporting without identifying individuals, so
  the community can see patterns without exposing private matters.
- **Policy changes are versioned.** Changes to moderation rules are
  proposed, reviewed, and released like any other governance change,
  never applied silently.

## Ownership and Review

- Maintainers own this policy and are accountable for applying it
  consistently to every contributor.
- Changes to this policy are proposed in a pull request that touches
  only the Governance/ folder, and are reviewed like any other
  governance change.
- This document is versioned with the repository; it describes the
  process the project actually follows rather than an aspiration.

## Success

This policy succeeds when moderation is fair and predictable; when
moderators use the smallest effective action and escalate only with
cause; when every decision names its reason and appeal route; and when
the community can verify activity through notices and aggregate
reporting without private details leaking.
