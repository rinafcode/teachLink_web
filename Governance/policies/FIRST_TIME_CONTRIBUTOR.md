# First-Time Contributor Policy

## Purpose

TeachLink welcomes contributors who are making their first open-source contribution or their first contribution to this project. This policy defines the support available to them, how suitable work is reserved, and what contributors and mentors can expect from one another.

The policy supplements the repository's `CONTRIBUTING.md`, Code of Conduct, and review policies. It does not lower quality, security, testing, or licensing requirements.

## Who is a first-time contributor?

For this policy, a first-time contributor is someone who:

- has never had a pull request merged into a public open-source project; or
- has contributed elsewhere but has not previously contributed to TeachLink.

Contributors may self-identify when applying for an issue. No proof is required.

## Reserved good-first issues

Maintainers should keep a small, healthy pool of issues labeled `good first issue` for newcomers. A reserved issue must:

- have a clear problem statement, bounded scope, and acceptance criteria;
- identify the expected files or subsystem when known;
- avoid unresolved architectural decisions and hidden dependencies;
- include setup or validation guidance appropriate to the task; and
- be small enough to complete and review without requiring privileged access.

When demand is high, maintainers may reserve selected good-first issues for first-time contributors for up to seven days after publication. The reservation must be stated on the issue. After the reservation expires, the issue may be assigned to any qualified contributor.

Assignment is explicit. Applying or commenting does not grant ownership of an issue. Contributors should wait for assignment before beginning work and should post a progress update if they become blocked.

## Support offered

First-time contributors may request:

- help reproducing the issue and setting up the development environment;
- clarification of acceptance criteria and repository conventions;
- pointers to relevant code, tests, and documentation;
- an early review of the proposed approach before substantial implementation;
- help interpreting CI failures that are specific to the repository; and
- constructive review feedback that distinguishes blocking changes from optional suggestions.

Questions asked in good faith are welcome. Contributors should use the issue or pull-request thread so answers remain visible to future contributors, while avoiding the disclosure of secrets or sensitive information.

## Mentorship expectations

When a maintainer or experienced contributor agrees to mentor an issue, they should:

1. acknowledge the contributor within the normal review SLA;
2. confirm the task boundary and highlight known risks or dependencies;
3. answer focused questions or redirect them to existing documentation;
4. review an early draft when that will prevent avoidable rework;
5. explain requested changes and identify which are required for merge; and
6. avoid taking over the implementation unless the contributor asks for help or the issue becomes urgent.

Mentorship provides guidance, not guaranteed synchronous support, guaranteed merge, or completion of the contributor's work. Mentors must follow the Code of Conduct and must not request credentials, private data, or work outside the assigned issue.

## Contributor expectations

First-time contributors are expected to:

- read `CONTRIBUTING.md` and the linked project policies;
- keep changes within the assigned scope;
- communicate blockers and significant delays promptly;
- run the documented checks or clearly state which checks could not be run;
- respond to review feedback respectfully; and
- avoid submitting copied, unlicensed, fabricated, or unverifiable work.

If a contributor is inactive for seven days without an update, a maintainer may release the assignment after one public reminder so another contributor can proceed.

## Review and recognition

First-time contributions are reviewed against the same functional, security, accessibility, and licensing standards as other contributions. Reviewers should prefer clear explanations and actionable examples over unexplained rejection.

Maintainers should recognize successful first contributions in the same way as other project contributions and, where appropriate, suggest a reasonable next issue that builds on the contributor's new project knowledge.

## Escalation

If a contributor cannot obtain clarification or believes this policy or the Code of Conduct is not being followed, they may request another maintainer's review on the issue. Conduct concerns should follow the repository's moderation and Code of Conduct processes.
