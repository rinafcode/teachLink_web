# Community Events Policy

## Purpose

This policy defines how community events related to TeachLink Web are proposed,
run, and supported. It lets contributors organise meetups, workshops,
hackathons, and online sessions that reflect the project's values, while
protecting attendees and keeping the project's name from being used carelessly.

## Scope

This policy applies to events that use the TeachLink Web name, are announced in
the project's official channels, or are presented as supported by the project,
whether in person or online. Independent events that do not claim project
backing are outside this policy, but their organisers are welcome to ask for
guidance. Use of the project name and logo follows the [Brand
Usage](../BRAND_USAGE.md).

## Proposing an Event

An organiser who wants project support opens an issue at least 30 days before
the event with:

- the purpose, format, expected audience, date, time zone, and location or
  platform;
- the organisers, their roles, and how attendees can contact them;
- the budget and any sponsors or funding sought, and how accessibility needs
  will be met;
- how the event will use the project's name, and who will represent the project,
  if anyone.
- Two maintainers review the proposal against this policy and the
  [Scope](../SCOPE.md), record approval or the reasons for declining, and agree
  what support the project will give (announcements, speaker time, recognition).
  Approval does not make the event an official project activity unless the
  approval says so.

## Code of Conduct Applicability

The [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) applies at every event that uses
the project's name:

- Organisers publish the Code of Conduct and a named contact for reports before
  registration opens, and brief staff and volunteers on how to respond.
- A report made at an event can be sent to the organisers on site and to the
  project through [Code of Conduct reporting](../COC_REPORTING.md); the
  project's enforcement procedures apply to participants who are also
  contributors.
- Organisers choose venues and platforms that are accessible, provide captions
  or equivalent for online sessions where possible, and state any registration
  or recording rules in advance.
- Organisers do not ask attendees for more personal data than they need, and
  handle it as described in the [Privacy Notice
  governance](../domains/PRIVACY_NOTICE.md).
- An event that does not follow the Code of Conduct may lose its approval and
  the right to use the project name.

## Sponsorship Rules

Funding must not compromise independence or trust:

- Sponsors are disclosed publicly on the event page, and sponsorship does not
  buy influence over project decisions, speaking slots on technical content, or
  review outcomes.
- Organisers follow the [Conflict of Interest
  policy](../policies/CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST.md) and tell maintainers of any
  relationship that could look like a conflict.
- Money raised in the project's name is handled transparently; where the
  project's funds are involved, the [Treasurer role](../roles/TREASURER.md) role
  applies.
- Sponsors may not use the event to collect attendee data for marketing without
  informed, separate consent (see the [Cookie and Consent
  governance](../domains/COOKIE_CONSENT.md) for the consent principles).
- After the event, organisers post a short summary: attendance, sponsors,
  expenses, and lessons learned.

## Ownership and Review

- Maintainers own this document and are accountable for applying it
  consistently.
- A maintainer keeps the events list and records approvals, withdrawals, and
  post-event summaries.
- Changes are proposed in a pull request that touches only the `Governance/`
  folder and are reviewed like any other governance change (see the [Review
  Policy](../policies/REVIEW_POLICY.md)).
- This document is versioned with the repository and describes how the project
  actually operates rather than an aspiration.

## Success

This policy succeeds when events that carry the project's name are safe,
accessible, and transparently funded, and when contributors know how to propose
one.
