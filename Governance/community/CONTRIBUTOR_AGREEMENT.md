# Contributor Agreement Overview

## Purpose

This document gives contributors a plain-language overview of what they agree to
when they contribute to TeachLink Web: the rights they grant, the promises they
make about their work, and how that agreement is shown and recorded. It
summarises the agreement; the governing text is the repository's licence and the
[DCO policy](../policies/DCO.md), which prevail if this overview differs.

## Scope

This overview applies to every contribution to the repository: code,
documentation, designs, translations, and other material submitted by pull
request, issue, or other means. It is not legal advice and does not create
obligations beyond the licence and sign-off terms. See the [Third-Party Licenses
policy](../policies/THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md) for how third-party material is
handled.

## What Contributors Agree To

By submitting a contribution, you confirm that:

- you wrote it, or you have the right to submit it under the repository's
  licence;
- you understand the contribution and a record of it (including your name and
  email in the commit history) will be public and permanent;
- your contribution is licensed under the same licence as the project, and you
  grant the project and its users the rights that licence provides, including
  the right to modify and redistribute it;
- if your employer or another party has rights in your work, you have permission
  to contribute it;
- you will not knowingly include code or content you cannot license, secrets, or
  personal data of others.
- You keep ownership of your contribution unless you agree otherwise, and you
  are credited as described in the [Recognition](../RECOGNITION.md).

## Sign-off Mechanism

Agreement is shown by signing off every commit:

- Add a `Signed-off-by: Your Name <you@example.com>` line to each commit, for
  example with `git commit -s`, using your real name or an established project
  identity and an email address you control.
- The sign-off certifies the Developer Certificate of Origin; the exact wording,
  how it is checked, and what happens when it is missing are set out in the [DCO
  policy](../policies/DCO.md).
- A pull request with commits missing a sign-off is not merged until it is
  fixed; maintainers will explain how to amend.
- Contributions made on behalf of an employer should be signed off by someone
  with authority to agree, and the employer can contact the maintainers to
  authorise their staff.
- Maintainers do not merge contributions they believe are not licensable.

## Where Records Are Kept

The agreement is evidenced in places that are public and durable:

- The commit history, including each sign-off, is the primary record of each
  contribution and its author.
- The pull request thread records the review, any questions about origin, and
  the maintainers' decision.
- Written authorisations from employers, and any permission to contribute
  material from another source, are kept by the maintainers as described in the
  record retention and legal hold policy.
- A contributor can ask the maintainers to correct their recorded name or email,
  or to withdraw a pending contribution. Merged contributions remain in the
  history because of how version control works, though material can be removed
  from future versions where there is a legal reason to do so.
- Changes to the agreement are made only through a governance pull request and
  never apply retroactively to contributions already accepted.

## Ownership and Review

- Maintainers own this document and are accountable for applying it
  consistently.
- Maintainers review this overview whenever the licence or the DCO policy
  changes.
- Changes are proposed in a pull request that touches only the `Governance/`
  folder and are reviewed like any other governance change (see the [Review
  Policy](../policies/REVIEW_POLICY.md)).
- This document is versioned with the repository and describes how the project
  actually operates rather than an aspiration.

## Success

This overview succeeds when a new contributor can read it in a few minutes and
understand what they are agreeing to, and when every contribution can be traced
to a signed-off commit.
