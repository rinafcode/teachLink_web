# Representation and Speaking Policy

## Purpose

This policy says who may represent TeachLink Web at conferences, meetups,
panels, and other public forums, how that is approved, and what must be
disclosed. It protects the project's reputation, helps contributors take
speaking opportunities fairly, and makes clear when a speaker is talking for the
project.

## Scope

This policy applies to talks, workshops, panels, interviews, and booth or
sponsorship appearances where a person is introduced as a TeachLink Web
maintainer, contributor, or representative. It covers the speaker's role only;
communication with journalists is governed by the press and media policy
(`MEDIA_POLICY.md`) and event hosting by the events policy (`EVENTS_POLICY.md`).

## Who May Represent the Project

Representing the project is open but deliberate:

- Any contributor in good standing may speak **about** TeachLink Web, accurately
  and in their own name.
- Speaking **on behalf of** the project (presenting a position, answering as the
  project's voice, or taking the project's slot at an event) is limited to
  maintainers and to contributors a maintainer has approved for that occasion.
- Invitations are shared with the community where practical so opportunities are
  spread fairly and newer contributors are given a chance to speak, especially
  when a maintainer cannot attend.
- A contributor who is subject to an active conduct sanction under the [Code of
  Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) does not represent the project.

## Approval Process

Before accepting a role that speaks for the project:

- Tell the maintainers in an issue or the project's designated channel at least
  14 days before the event, with the event, topic, date, audience, and any fee
  or travel support offered.
- Two maintainers approve the topic and role, check for conflicts of interest
  under the [Conflict of Interest policy](../policies/CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST.md),
  and record the decision; no response in the review window means the speaker
  may speak about the project but not for it.
- Slides and demos that present the project's roadmap, security matters, or
  numbers about users are checked against what the project has already
  published.
- After the event, the speaker may share a short summary and slides with the
  community, which counts toward [Recognition](../RECOGNITION.md).

## Disclosure Rules

Speakers are open about their position and interests:

- State at the start whether you speak for the project or for yourself, and say
  if your employer, a sponsor, or an organiser pays for your travel or time.
- Disclose any financial interest in products or services you mention, in line
  with the [Conflict of Interest policy](../policies/CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST.md).
- Do not present unreleased features, private conversations, security issues, or
  personal data of contributors or users.
- Use the project name and logo only as the [Brand Usage](../BRAND_USAGE.md)
  allows, and follow the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) in all
  dealings with organisers and audiences.
- A speaker who finds that the event does not meet the project's standards for
  safety or conduct may withdraw and tell the maintainers.

## Ownership and Review

- Maintainers own this document and are accountable for applying it
  consistently.
- A maintainer keeps the list of approved appearances and the disclosure
  statements given.
- Changes are proposed in a pull request that touches only the `Governance/`
  folder and are reviewed like any other governance change (see the [Review
  Policy](../policies/REVIEW_POLICY.md)).
- This document is versioned with the repository and describes how the project
  actually operates rather than an aspiration.

## Success

This policy succeeds when audiences know who is speaking for the project, when
speaking opportunities are shared fairly, and when no one misrepresents the
project's positions.
