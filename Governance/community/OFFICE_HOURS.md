# Office Hours Policy

## Purpose

This policy sets up regular community office hours for TeachLink Web: open
sessions where contributors and users can talk to maintainers about
contributions, designs, and questions that are hard to settle in an issue
thread. It defines the format so sessions are predictable, welcoming, and
transparent.

## Scope

This policy applies to the community office hours sessions and the channels used
to organise them. It does not replace public decision-making: office hours are
for discussion and help, and decisions made in a session are recorded in the
usual place (see the [RFC process](../processes/RFC_PROCESS.md) and the
[Communication Norms](../policies/COMMUNICATION_NORMS.md)).

## Schedule and Format

Sessions are designed to be easy to attend:

- Sessions are held on a regular cadence, at least monthly, with the date, time
  (in UTC), and joining details announced in the project's official channels at
  least 7 days ahead. Where possible the time alternates to suit different time
  zones.
- Each session lasts up to 60 minutes: an introduction and agenda, topics in the
  order submitted, and open questions at the end.
- Sessions follow the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) and are open to
  anyone. Accessibility is considered: the joining method works without special
  software where possible, and notes are available for those who cannot attend.
- Sessions are not recorded unless every attendee is told in advance and the
  host agrees; written notes are the default record.
- A session may be cancelled with notice if no topics are submitted or no host
  is available.

## Hosts

Hosting is shared:

- Hosts are maintainers or trusted contributors who volunteer; at least one
  maintainer is present, and the host role rotates so no one person carries it.
- The host keeps to time, ensures everyone who wants to speak can, moves
  unresolved topics to an issue or discussion, and enforces the [Code of
  Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md).
- A host who has a conflict of interest in a topic hands that item to another
  host (see the [Conflict of Interest
  policy](../policies/CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST.md)).
- Hosts publish short notes after each session: attendees who consent to being
  named, topics covered, and any follow-up actions with owners.

## Submitting Topics

Anyone can propose a topic:

- Topics are submitted at least 48 hours ahead through a comment on the
  session's announcement issue or discussion, with a short description and a
  link to any related issue or pull request.
- Good topics are questions about contributing, requests for design feedback,
  help getting unstuck, and community ideas. Security vulnerabilities must not
  be raised in a session; they follow the [Security
  Policy](../SECURITY_POLICY.md).
- Items are taken in submission order, with newcomer questions given priority.
  Same-day topics are taken if time remains.
- Anything that needs a decision is turned into a public issue or an [RFC
  process](../processes/RFC_PROCESS.md) rather than decided in the room.

## Ownership and Review

- Maintainers own this document and are accountable for applying it
  consistently.
- A maintainer maintains the schedule, host rotation, and archive of session
  notes.
- Changes are proposed in a pull request that touches only the `Governance/`
  folder and are reviewed like any other governance change (see the [Review
  Policy](../policies/REVIEW_POLICY.md)).
- This document is versioned with the repository and describes how the project
  actually operates rather than an aspiration.

## Success

This policy succeeds when sessions run on schedule, when newcomers get timely
answers, and when every outcome is recorded publicly so those who could not
attend are not left behind.
