# Ambassador Program Policy

## Purpose

This policy establishes an ambassador program for TeachLink Web: community
members who help others discover, understand, and contribute to the project in
their own regions, languages, and communities. It states what ambassadors do,
how they are chosen, and how their work is recognised, so the program grows
trust rather than confusion about who speaks for the project.

## Scope

This policy applies to people who hold or apply for the ambassador title.
Ambassadors are community advocates, not maintainers: the title carries no merge
rights, no authority over governance decisions, and no authority to speak
officially for the project beyond the limits below. Official statements remain
governed by the press and media and social media policies, and the use of
project names and logos by the [Brand Usage](../BRAND_USAGE.md).

## Ambassador Responsibilities

Ambassadors volunteer time to:

- welcome newcomers and point them to the [first-time contributor
  guidance](../policies/FIRST_TIME_CONTRIBUTOR.md) and the [onboarding
  process](../processes/ONBOARDING.md);
- share what the project does accurately, including its limits, and correct
  misunderstandings they encounter;
- help organise or support local meetups, workshops, and translation or
  documentation efforts in their community;
- collect feedback from their community and bring it to maintainers through
  public issues or discussions;
- model the standards of the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) and
  [Communication Norms](../policies/COMMUNICATION_NORMS.md).
- Ambassadors do not promise features, release dates, or support, do not handle
  security reports (those follow the [Security Policy](../SECURITY_POLICY.md)),
  and do not accept money or gifts in the project's name.

## Selection

Ambassadors are selected openly and fairly:

- **Eligibility.** A candidate has at least 3 months of constructive
  participation, is in good standing under the [Code of
  Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), and has no unresolved conduct case.
- **Nomination.** A candidate may self-nominate or be nominated by a
  contributor, with a short statement of the community they would serve and what
  they would do.
- **Decision.** Two maintainers review the nomination, check for conflicts of
  interest under the [Conflict of Interest
  policy](../policies/CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST.md), and record the decision and
  reasons. The candidate is told the outcome either way.
- **Term.** A term is 12 months and can be renewed after a short check-in. An
  ambassador may step down at any time.
- An ambassador who becomes inactive for 6 months, or who breaches the [Code of
  Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) or this policy, is removed from the program
  through the same two-maintainer process, with a chance to respond.

## Recognition

The project recognises ambassadors without creating unequal status:

- Ambassadors are listed in the contributor recognition materials described in
  [Recognition](../RECOGNITION.md), with their consent.
- They may use the title "TeachLink Web Community Ambassador" while in good
  standing, following the [Brand Usage](../BRAND_USAGE.md).
- Maintainers thank ambassadors publicly for specific contributions, such as
  events held or translations completed.
- Recognition is for effort and impact, not for follower counts or the number of
  people recruited, and is never tied to payment or perks that could create a
  conflict of interest.

## Ownership and Review

- Maintainers own this document and are accountable for applying it
  consistently.
- A maintainer acts as program coordinator, keeps the list of current
  ambassadors and their terms, and runs the annual review.
- Changes are proposed in a pull request that touches only the `Governance/`
  folder and are reviewed like any other governance change (see the [Review
  Policy](../policies/REVIEW_POLICY.md)).
- This document is versioned with the repository and describes how the project
  actually operates rather than an aspiration.

## Success

This policy succeeds when newcomers meet knowledgeable, welcoming people in
their own communities, when ambassadors are clearly understood not to speak
officially for the project, and when selection and removal are transparent and
fair.
