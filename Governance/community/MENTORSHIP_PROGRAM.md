# Mentorship Program Policy

## Purpose

This policy sets up a voluntary mentorship program that helps newer contributors
grow into effective members of the TeachLink Web community. It states how
mentors and mentees are matched, what each can expect, and how long a mentorship
lasts, so participation is fair, safe, and useful to both sides.

## Scope

This policy applies to contributors who take part in the mentorship program as
mentors or mentees. It does not replace the [onboarding
process](../processes/ONBOARDING.md) every contributor receives, the [Review
Policy](../policies/REVIEW_POLICY.md) that applies to all changes, or the [Code
of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), which governs every mentorship interaction.

## Matching Mentors and Mentees

Matching is intended to be simple, voluntary, and fair:

- **Mentors** are contributors with a record of merged contributions and good
  standing under the [Code of Conduct](../CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md), who volunteer and
  are confirmed by a maintainer.
- **Mentees** are contributors who ask to join and describe their goals, such as
  learning an area of the codebase or growing toward a project role.
- Matches are made by a maintainer or program coordinator using the mentee's
  goals, the mentor's expertise, time zone and availability, and spoken
  languages.
- A mentor does not mentor someone they have a conflict of interest with (see
  the [Conflict of Interest policy](../policies/CONFLICT_OF_INTEREST.md)) and
  does not mentor more people than they can support, normally no more than two
  at a time.
- Either party may decline a match or ask for a different one at any point
  without giving a reason.

## Expectations

Participants agree to the following:

- **Mentors** respond within an agreed time, share knowledge and context, give
  constructive feedback, point to project resources, and help mentees find good
  first work. Mentors do not do the mentee's work or guarantee roles or
  approvals.
- **Mentees** state their goals, come prepared, act on feedback, ask questions,
  and tell the mentor early if their availability changes.
- **Both** keep conversations respectful and professional, keep private matters
  private, and raise any concern about behaviour through [Code of Conduct
  reporting](../COC_REPORTING.md).
- Mentorship happens in public project channels where possible; one-to-one
  conversations stay within the [Communication
  Norms](../policies/COMMUNICATION_NORMS.md).
- Mentorship never affects review outcomes: a mentor's relationship with a
  mentee does not exempt either from the normal independent review.

## Duration and Review

A mentorship has a defined shape:

- A standard mentorship lasts 3 months and can be extended once by up to 3
  months by mutual agreement.
- At the midpoint and at the end, the pair and the coordinator check progress
  against the mentee's goals and the experience of both sides.
- A mentorship ends early if either party asks, if availability is lost, or if a
  conduct concern is upheld.
- At the end, the mentee is encouraged to share what they learned, and the pair
  may be recognised under the [Recognition](../RECOGNITION.md).
- The program is reviewed annually using anonymous participant feedback; the
  results inform changes to this policy.

## Ownership and Review

- Maintainers own this document and are accountable for applying it
  consistently.
- A maintainer acts as program coordinator, keeps the list of participants, and
  handles matching and early-ending requests.
- Changes are proposed in a pull request that touches only the `Governance/`
  folder and are reviewed like any other governance change (see the [Review
  Policy](../policies/REVIEW_POLICY.md)).
- This document is versioned with the repository and describes how the project
  actually operates rather than an aspiration.

## Success

This policy succeeds when mentees reach their stated goals, mentors feel
supported rather than overloaded, and the program remains voluntary, respectful,
and open to new participants.
