# Code of Conduct

## Purpose

This document sets the standard of behavior expected of everyone
who takes part in TeachLink Web. It gives contributors, maintainers,
and users a clear, versioned reference for what the project expects,
what it does not accept, and where the standard applies, so that the
community stays welcoming, safe, and focused on building an
education platform that serves learners well.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all project spaces for this
repository, including issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions,
commit messages, the project wiki, chat channels, and any meeting or
event held on behalf of the project.

It also applies outside project spaces when a person is officially
representing the project, such as posting from a project account or
speaking at an event as a maintainer, and when conduct outside
project spaces creates a credible risk to the safety of a community
member inside them.

It applies equally to every participant: first-time contributors,
regular contributors, maintainers, and project leadership. Holding a
role never exempts anyone from this standard.

## Expected Behavior

Every participant is expected to:

- **Be respectful.** Treat others with courtesy, including when you
  disagree, and assume good intent until shown otherwise.
- **Be inclusive.** Welcome people of every background, identity,
  and level of experience, and use language that does not exclude.
- **Be constructive.** Critique ideas and code, not people. Give
  feedback that is specific and actionable, and accept it graciously.
- **Be patient with newcomers.** Answer questions kindly and point
  people to documentation rather than dismissing them.
- **Respect privacy.** Keep private information private and ask
  before sharing anything another person told you in confidence.
- **Take responsibility.** Acknowledge mistakes, apologize to those
  affected, and learn from the experience.
- **Look out for others.** Report behavior that breaks this Code of
  Conduct, as described in `Governance/COC_REPORTING.md`.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not accepted in any project space:

- **Harassment.** Unwelcome conduct directed at a person, including
  offensive comments about identity, appearance, or background.
- **Discrimination.** Excluding or demeaning people because of age,
  disability, ethnicity, gender identity or expression, nationality,
  race, religion, sexual orientation, or any similar characteristic.
- **Threats and violent language.** Threatening violence against any
  person, or encouraging others to do so, including self-harm.
- **Personal attacks.** Insults, name-calling, trolling, and
  deliberately inflammatory or derogatory comments.
- **Sexualized content.** Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome
  sexual attention or advances.
- **Doxxing.** Publishing another person's private information, such
  as an address, contact details, or real name, without permission.
- **Sustained disruption.** Deliberately derailing discussions,
  spamming, or repeatedly ignoring a maintainer's request to stop.
- **Retaliation.** Punishing or threatening anyone for reporting a
  concern or for taking part in an investigation or appeal.

Behavior not listed here may still be unacceptable when it harms the
safety or inclusion of community members. The list illustrates the
standard; it does not limit it.

## Reporting and Enforcement

- Concerns are reported through the private channel set out in
  `Governance/COC_REPORTING.md`, which also defines the response
  timeline and the confidentiality guarantee.
- Violations are addressed through the graduated steps in
  `Governance/COC_ENFORCEMENT.md`.
- Enforcement decisions may be appealed as described in
  `Governance/COC_APPEALS.md`.
- Moderation of individual comments follows
  `Governance/policies/MODERATION.md`.

## Ownership and Review

- Maintainers own this Code of Conduct and are responsible for
  upholding it, as set out in `Governance/roles/MAINTAINER.md`.
- Changes to this Code of Conduct are proposed in a pull request that
  touches only the Governance/ folder.
- This governance document is versioned with the repository; it
  describes the standard the project actually applies.

## Success

This Code of Conduct succeeds when every participant knows what is
expected of them; when people who experience or witness unacceptable
behavior feel safe reporting it; when enforcement is consistent and
fair; and when newcomers choose to stay and contribute.

## Regression Tests
